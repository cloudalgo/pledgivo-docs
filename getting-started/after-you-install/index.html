    Getting Started

    After you install#

    The install seeds everything it is allowed to seed. Seven things are left, and several of them can only be done by a human in Salesforce Setup. This page is the written version of the Get Started panel inside the app — every screenshot below was taken while completing the setup by hand in a brand-new org.

    You were probably sent here automatically

    Salesforce redirects you to this page as soon as the package finishes installing or upgrading. Nothing is broken — this is the handover.

    Watch it first#

    What it is. A narrated 7-minute-46 walkthrough of the whole setup, from the empty Get Started panel to 7/7 — your org is ready to take donations. It is built from the 70 numbered screenshots on this page rather than recorded live, so nothing moves faster than you can read: each screen holds for as long as its narration takes, an arrow marks the exact control being clicked, and the banner underneath names the action and where in Salesforce you are. Captions are available in the player's subtitles menu if you want them, and the chapter list above jumps straight to a step. Pause anywhere — the same screenshot appears further down the page with the full written explanation.

    The seven steps#

    The Get Started panel (App Launcher → Pledgivo → Settings) is the same checklist, checked against your org live. A step there is never a box you tick — it reports what your org actually looks like right now, so if someone later deactivates the last payment account or aborts the scheduled jobs, the step reopens on its own.

    # Step Where the work happens
    1 Give your team access Salesforce Setup
    2 Confirm record type mapping Settings console
    3 Publish your donation site Setup + Experience Builder + Workspaces
    4 Connect your Stripe account Salesforce Setup
    5 Let your donation site use the payment credential Salesforce Setup
    6 Start the background jobs Already done by the install
    7 Set your organization name and logo Settings console

    An eighth item — Trust the content hosts you actually use — is listed but never counts against your seven. See Third-party content.

    What it shows. The panel on a brand-new install. One step is already green — the install seeds the record type mappings and schedules the jobs for you. Everything else is waiting on a human.

    7 steps 5 need Salesforce Setup ~30 minutes

    1. Permission sets#

    Nobody can see a campaign, a donation, or the Settings console without one of the packaged permission sets. The install cannot assign them for you — Salesforce gives a post-install script no way to know who on your team does what.

    Permission set Give it to
    Fundraising_Admin Whoever runs fundraising. Full CRUD on every object, plus the Settings console.
    Fundraising_User The rest of the fundraising team. Day-to-day record access, no setup.
    Fundraising_ReadOnly Anyone who needs to see gifts but never change them — finance, leadership.
    Fundraising_GuestDonor Not a person. Assign to your Experience Cloud site's guest user only. Least-privilege grants for the public donation flow.

    What it shows. Setup → Permission Sets, filtered to the packaged sets. All four arrive with the install; none of them is assigned to anyone yet.

    What it shows. The Fundraising_Admin detail page. This is where you confirm you are looking at the packaged set (it carries the package namespace) rather than a lookalike of your own.

    What it shows. Manage Assignments from the permission set — useful for auditing who already holds it, which here is nobody. Assigning from this direction is fine when it works, but the route below is the one that behaves consistently on a fresh org, so that is what the rest of these screenshots follow.

    The route that always works is from the user, not from the permission set:

    1. Setup → Users → Users → click the person's name.
    2. Scroll to the Permission Set Assignments related list.
    3. Edit Assignments.
    4. Move the set from Available Permission Sets to Enabled Permission Sets → Save.

    What it shows. The Permission Set Assignments related list on the user record, with the Edit Assignments button. This list is also the fastest way to answer "why can't this person see the console?"

    What it shows. The two-column picker. Every permission set in the org appears on the left, including ones from other packages — read the name carefully before adding.

    What it shows. Fundraising_Admin selected on the left, before it is moved across.

    What it shows. The set moved into Enabled Permission Sets. Nothing is committed until you press Save.

    What it shows. Saved. The assignment now appears on the user record — this is the state the Get Started panel reads.

    What it shows. Back in the console, Give your team access has flipped to DONE with the evidence line "1 user(s) hold Fundraising Admin or Fundraising User". Nothing was ticked by hand — the panel re-queried the org.

    The Settings console is Fundraising_Admin only. A Fundraising_User cannot open it, and cannot change what it configures by another route either: payment accounts, page designs, and every app setting are read-only for that set. They still see those records — picking a design or a payment account for a campaign keeps working — they just cannot edit them from a record page, the API, or Data Loader. If someone on your team needs to configure payments, designs, or org settings, give them Fundraising_Admin; there is no middle tier.

    2. Record types#

    The app writes donations, events, and tributes onto Opportunity and Campaign record types. The install seeds a default mapping of seven records pointing at the record types it ships, so this step is normally already green.

    What it shows. Settings console → Record Types, with all seven mapping records present. Change these only if your org already uses its own record types for gifts and you want the app to write to those instead.

    Change this before you take gifts, not after

    Remapping later does not move donations that were already written under the old record type. Decide now if you are going to.

    3. The Experience site#

    Donors give on an Experience Cloud site, not inside Salesforce. Internal-only use — staff recording gifts taken over the phone — does not need one; anything public does.

    This step has three distinct parts, and skipping the third is the single most common reason a donation link 404s: create the site, enable public access and publish it, then activate it. Publishing and activating are different operations in different screens.

    Create the site#

    If Setup has no Digital Experiences menu, switch the feature on first: Setup → Digital Experiences → Settings → Enable Digital Experiences. It is org-wide, asks you to pick an Enhanced Domain subdomain, and cannot be undone.

    What it shows. Settings console → Site & Domain. Steps the app can verify against your org are marked Done or Pending; steps Salesforce exposes no API for are marked Can't verify and are yours to confirm.

    What it shows. Setup → Digital Experiences → All Sites before any site exists. New starts the template gallery.

    What it shows. The template gallery. Most of these are Aura templates the package does not target.

    What it shows. Build Your Own (LWR) — the one to pick. The package's public components are Lightning Web Runtime components; they cannot be dropped onto an Aura template.

    What it shows. Naming the site and choosing its URL path. The path becomes part of every public donation link, so pick something short and permanent — /fundraising, not /fundraising-2026-test.

    What it shows. Experience Workspaces — reached from the Workspaces link next to the site in All Sites. Builder is where you design pages; Administration is where you activate. You will need both.

    What it shows. Administration → Settings. A brand-new site starts at status Preview, which means logged-in admins can see it and the public cannot.

    Enable public access and publish#

    What it shows. Experience Builder → the gear icon → Settings → General. Template, public access, published status and the guest user profile all live on this one screen.

    What it shows. Guest users can see and interact with the site without logging in, ticked. Without this, the donation page serves a login screen to donors.

    What it shows. The Publish dialog, confirming the URL your changes will go to. The description field is optional and feeds the Change History panel.

    What it shows. Publishing is asynchronous — Salesforce emails you when it finishes. Wait for that email before making further changes.

    Activate it#

    Published is not the same as Live

    A published site whose status is still Preview serves nothing to the public. The Activate button is not in Experience Builder — it is in Workspaces → Administration → Settings, and it is the only thing that moves the site to Live. The Get Started panel shows this step as Almost until then.

    Because activation is the last thing that happens in a real setup — you publish early and activate once everything else works — it is covered at the end of this page, in Finish step 3: activate the site.

    Full walkthrough: Set up your Experience Cloud site.

    4. Connect Stripe#

    This package never ships, stores, or creates a payment credential of its own. You build the External Credential and Named Credential yourself in Setup — that is where your Stripe secret key lives, encrypted, and it never touches a custom field or an Apex method.

    You build two pairs, not one. This is deliberate and it is the security boundary of the whole integration:

    Pair Key to use Who is allowed to reach it
    Public — Stripe_Auth + Stripe_Public A restricted key that can create charges and nothing else Staff and the site's guest user
    Admin — Stripe_Admin_Auth + Stripe_Admin Your full secret key — refunds, lookups Staff only. Never the guest user

    What it shows. Settings console → Payments. Ten steps with the exact values to paste, plus a live connection test at the end. The console guides the build; it never asks you to type a secret into it.

    What it shows. The reference values, per step, with copy affordances — names, protocol, parameter name, and the exact merge-field syntax for the header. Keep this open in a second tab while you work in Setup.

    The public external credential#

    What it shows. Setup → Security → Named Credentials → External Credentials, before anything exists. External credentials hold the secret; named credentials hold the URL.

    What it shows. New on the External Credentials tab.

    What it shows. Label Stripe Public, name Stripe_Auth, authentication protocol Custom. Custom is correct here — Stripe authenticates with a bearer token, not OAuth.

    What it shows. Saved. The Principals and Custom Headers related lists only appear after the first save, which is why this is two steps and not one.

    What it shows. The principal: parameter name OrgPrincipal, sequence 1, identity type Named Principal, and one authentication parameter named SecretKey whose value is Bearer sk_…. The word Bearer and a space must precede the key — the header is passed through verbatim.

    What it shows. Saved. The value is now write-only — Salesforce will never display it again, to you or to any Apex code. If you need to change the key, you overwrite it; you cannot read it back to check.

    What it shows. New under Custom Headers.

    What it shows. Header name Authorization, value {!$Credential.Stripe_Auth.SecretKey}. This merge field is how the encrypted secret reaches Stripe without any code ever holding it. The name in the middle must match your external credential's API name exactly.

    What it shows. The finished public external credential — one principal, one header.

    The public named credential#

    What it shows. The Named Credentials tab, still empty. The external credential you just built is not usable on its own.

    What it shows. New on the Named Credentials tab.

    What it shows. Label Stripe Public, name Stripe_Public, URL https://api.stripe.com (no trailing path), external credential Stripe Public.

    What it shows. The callout options, which matter more than they look. Allow Formulas in HTTP Header must be on or your merge field is sent as literal text. Generate Authorization Header must be off or Salesforce overwrites your header with its own and Stripe returns 401.

    The admin pair#

    Repeat all of the above with the admin names and your full secret key.

    What it shows. The second external credential, Stripe_Admin_Auth — identical in shape, different key.

    What it shows. Its own OrgPrincipal. The two credentials share no state — that is the point. A leak of one does not expose the other.

    What it shows. The admin named credential, same URL, same callout options.

    What it shows. Both named credentials in the list. This is the finished state of step 4.

    What it shows. Back in the console, Re-check re-reads the org against the finished build.

    Add the payment account#

    A named credential is plumbing. The Payment Account record is what a campaign actually charges through — until an active one exists, the donation form cannot take money.

    What it shows. Settings console → Payment Accounts.

    What it shows. One account pointing at both named credentials — the public one for charges, the admin one for refunds — and marked active. The label is yours; "Stripe (test mode)" is a useful convention while you are still on test keys.

    What it shows. Saved and active.

    Full walkthrough: Connecting Stripe.

    5. Grant the Stripe credential#

    Two permission sets you create yourself

    This is the step that catches people out. A credential nobody is granted is a credential nobody can use — and granting it to your Experience Cloud site's guest user widens what an anonymous visitor can reach, so it stays your decision to make. The package never does it.

    Public donations fail at the payment step until you do it. Everything else on the page works, which is exactly what makes it easy to miss.

    Why the permission set can't be part of the package

    Salesforce does not allow a managed permission set to grant access to an external credential principal — the API rejects it outright. That's why these sets have to be ones you create in your own org, and why Fundraising_Admin can never ship with the grant on it.

    What it shows. Setup → Permission Sets → New. This set exists for one purpose: to carry a principal grant.

    What it shows. Name it after what it grants, and keep public and admin in separate sets — you are about to assign one of them to an anonymous user and not the other.

    What it shows. Created, with no grants on it yet.

    What it shows. On the permission set: Apps → External Credential Principal Access → Edit. This page is easy to miss — it is not under Object Settings or System Permissions.

    What it shows. The public credential's OrgPrincipal enabled. Check the credential name in the row — the admin principal looks almost identical in this list.

    What it shows. Saved.

    What it shows. The same edit on the second set, granting the admin principal. This set must never reach a guest user.

    Assign them#

    What it shows. The site's guest user. Reach it from Experience Builder → Settings → General → the guest user profile link, or from Setup → Users. Its name is derived from the site name, and it is a real user record with real permission set assignments.

    What it shows. The public payments set being assigned to the guest user — and only that one.

    What it shows. Saved. From this moment a public donation can reach Stripe.

    What it shows. Staff holding the admin set — this is what makes refunds work. Give it to the people who take payments and issue refunds, and nobody else.

    Don't forget the guest user — and don't give it the admin credential

    Assigning to staff but not to the site guest user is the failure mode that looks healthiest: internal testing works, and every public donation fails at payment.

    The opposite mistake is worse and just as quiet: putting the guest user on the permission set that grants the admin credential lets an anonymous visitor's session reach a full secret key, refunds included.

    Allow the package's namespace to make the callout#

    Easy to miss — the credential looks finished without it

    A named credential built by an admin is, by default, invisible to managed-package code. Salesforce gates this with a field on the named credential called Allowed Namespaces for Callouts: until the package's namespace is listed there, every callout the package attempts through that credential is blocked, no matter how correct the credential itself is.

    Do this on both named credentials.

    1. Setup → Named Credentials → open Stripe_Public.
    2. Edit → find Allowed Namespaces for Callouts (a plain comma-separated text field).
    3. Enter the package namespace → Save.
    4. Repeat for Stripe_Admin.

    What it shows. The field on the edit form, with the package namespace typed in. It is free text with a namespaceA, namespaceB placeholder — there is no picker, and a typo fails silently at callout time rather than at save time.

    What it shows. Saved. The namespace now appears under Managed Package Access on the credential's detail page — that section is your confirmation the grant took.

    What it shows. The end of the Payments guide: Prove it works makes a real callout to Stripe under the credential you just built and reports the account id Stripe answered with. This is the only check that exercises the whole chain — key, header, callout options and namespace grant — end to end. If this passes, the credential is correct regardless of what any other check says.

    6. Background jobs#

    Donations are finalized, payments swept, refunds reconciled, and campaign totals repaired by scheduled Apex — 21 cron entries in total. The install schedules all of them, so this step arrives green and there is nothing to do.

    You only need it if some were aborted (a deployment that touched a scheduled class will do it). Settings console → Get Started → Schedule the missing jobs, or Scheduled Jobs for the per-job breakdown and on-demand runs.

    Family Count Cadence
    Finalizer-<m> 12 Every 5 minutes
    ReconciliationHeartbeat-<m> 4 Every 15 minutes
    Recurring donations, retention purge 2 Daily
    StripePaymentSweep, RefundReconcile 2 Hourly
    CampaignRollup 1 Nightly

    7. Organization name and logo#

    Your organization name and logo appear on the donation form, the receipt email, and the donor portal. The install seeds a placeholder logo so nothing renders broken on day one — it is not your logo.

    What it shows. Five down. The panel names what is left rather than making you compare lists.

    What it shows. Settings console → Organization & Donations, untouched. This panel holds your identity fields (display name, Tax ID, logo) and your gift rules (minimum and maximum amount, suggested amounts, processing-fee percentage and fixed portion, donation page path, donor portal link expiry, default thank-you button). There is no mailing address field here — receipts take their address from your Salesforce company information.

    What it shows. Display name and Tax ID (EIN) saved. The display name is what a donor reads on the form and the receipt — set it before you send anyone a link.

    Full walkthrough: Brand your receipt and notification emails.

    Finish step 3: activate the site#

    Back to the one step publishing did not finish.

    What it shows. Setup → All Sites. The site exists, has a real public URL, and its status is Preview — visible to you, invisible to donors.

    What it shows. Experience Builder → Settings → General, confirming public access is on and Published Status already reads Published with the live URL. Everything here is correct — and the site is still not reachable by the public. This screen has no Activate control.

    What it shows. Publishing again after the page changes, from the Publish button in the top-right of Builder.

    What it shows. "We're publishing your changes now." Publishing succeeded — and the site's status is still Preview.

    What it shows. Workspaces, reached from the Workspaces link beside the site in Setup → All Sites. The header reads "Fundraising ( Preview · Published )" — the two states side by side. The tile you need is Administration.

    What it shows. Administration → Settings. Here — and only here — is the Activate button, sitting next to the status. Salesforce warns that activating sends a welcome email to members; you can suppress that beforehand under Emails.

    What it shows. Activated. The status is now Live, and the public URL serves the site to anyone.

    What it shows. 7/7 — Your org is ready to take donations. Every step was verified against the org, not ticked. The note underneath is the important part: "Steps stay live — if someone deactivates the last payment account or aborts the jobs, this reopens."

    Third-party content#

    Optional — nothing here is required

    During the install Salesforce shows you an Approve Third-Party Access dialog listing every external domain the package wants your org to trust. This package lists Stripe and nothing else:

    Domain Why
    js.stripe.com Loads Stripe.js, which collects the card details
    api.stripe.com The payment API the browser confirms against
    m.stripe.com, m.stripe.network Stripe's fraud-signal endpoints — payments fail without them
    hooks.stripe.com 3-D Secure / bank authentication screens

    That is deliberate. A fundraising package asking to reach a list of unfamiliar domains reads as a warning rather than as a payment integration, so the optional features that use other hosts ship without their trusted sites. You add those yourself, only if you want the feature.

    Add this domain Directive Unlocks
    https://images.unsplash.com Image Using an Unsplash photo as a campaign hero or social card
    https://www.youtube.com Frame A YouTube video in a campaign page video block
    https://www.youtube-nocookie.com Frame YouTube privacy-enhanced (no-cookie) embeds
    https://player.vimeo.com Frame A Vimeo video in a campaign page video block

    To add one: Setup → Security → CSP Trusted Sites → New Trusted Site. Enter the URL exactly as written above, name it anything, tick the directive shown, leave Context as All, and save.

    The Get Started panel's Trust the content hosts you actually use step reads your org's trusted sites live and shows which of the four you have. It never counts against your 7 steps and it never offers a fix button — Salesforce does not let Apex create a trusted site, so this one is yours.

    Your campaigns start with a packaged hero image, not a stock photo

    The package ships its own artwork — a hero backdrop and a social share card, both drawn from the brand palette rather than licensed from a stock library — and publishes them into your org at install time. Your page designs are stamped with them automatically, so a campaign looks finished on day one.

    They are served from your own org's file domain, which browsers allow without a trusted site, so nothing on the list above is needed to see them. Replace either one at any time by uploading your own image in Designs; an image you have chosen is never overwritten by a package upgrade. To paste a URL from an outside host instead — Unsplash, your own CDN — that host needs a CSP trusted site first, per the table above.

    When you're done#

    The Get Started panel shows 7/7 and "Your org is ready to take donations." Setup is finished — but your org still has nothing to give to.

    Everything you raise money with lives in the Fundraisers tab#

    Campaigns and ticketed events are not created in Settings. Open the Fundraisers tab (App Launcher → Pledgivo → Fundraisers, or the Open Fundraisers button on the finished Get Started panel). It is the console your fundraising team works in day to day, and it is the only place a public donation page comes from.

    In the Fundraisers tab What you get
    New fundraiser wizard A Campaign, step by step: goal and fund, designations, page design, and its public URL.
    Ticket types Turns a campaign into a ticketed event — tiers, prices, capacity, and the registration page.
    The fundraiser list Search, filter, publish, or deactivate anything already live.
    A fundraiser's detail page Its donations, registrations, attendees, questions, and FAQs in one place.

    The Settings console you just finished is for org-wide configuration — payments, receipts, branding, jobs. You will rarely go back to it. The Fundraisers tab is where the actual work happens.

    Full walkthrough: Set up your first campaign, or Set up a ticketed event.

    Then prove it end to end#

    Once your first campaign is published, send yourself a real gift for the smallest amount Stripe allows and refund it. It is the only way to prove the whole path — form, payment, receipt, refund — works in your org.
