The usual arrangement

A separate giving platform costs you twice.

Once as a percentage of every gift. Again in the hours your team spends getting those gifts back into Salesforce and cleaning up after the sync.

With a separate giving platform 5 hand-offs

  1. Donor gives on a page hosted outside your org.
  2. The platform keeps a percentage, on top of card processing.
  3. A connector copies the gift into Salesforce on a schedule.
  4. Someone merges the duplicate donor it created.
  5. Reports are right once the sync has caught up.
3% of $250kis $7,500 a year that never reaches the mission.

With Pledgivo 1 system

  1. Donor gives on your own Experience Cloud site.
  2. Stripe charges its card fee. Nothing else is taken.
  3. The gift, donor and receipt are written in your org.
  4. Returning donors are matched by email, so they stay one record.
  5. Reports are right as soon as the gift is.

How a gift travels

Three steps, and none of them leave your org.

The usual weak points (a public webhook, a sync job, a second copy of your donors) don't exist here.

01 · Your site

The donor gives

Stripe.js turns the card into a token in the browser. No card number ever reaches Salesforce.

02 · Your org

Salesforce confirms

Your org asks Stripe what happened, instead of waiting on an inbound webhook you'd have to secure.

03 · Your records

Records land

An Opportunity, a donor and a receipt, reportable with standard Salesforce reports right away.

Your Stripe secret key lives in a Named Credential you build in Setup. The package never ships, stores or creates a payment credential of its own.

Built for security review

Your donor records stay in your org.

Pledgivo runs entirely inside Salesforce. Your IT team reviews one package instead of a second platform.

Card numbers stay with Stripe

Stripe.js tokenizes cards in the donor's browser. Only a token reaches Apex.

Keys stay in Named Credentials

You create them in Setup. The package never stores a secret in a field.

Your permissions apply

Staff see what your sharing rules and permission sets allow, enforced in the code.

No external scripts in your org

No component loads code from another site. Stripe's own payment script is the one documented exception.
