    Getting Started

    After you install#

    The install seeds everything it is allowed to seed. Seven things are left, and two of them can only be done by a human in Salesforce Setup. This page is the written version of the Get Started panel inside the app — work through either one.

    You were probably sent here automatically

    Salesforce redirects you to this page as soon as the package finishes installing or upgrading. Nothing is broken — this is the handover.

    Do this in the app instead, if you can#

    Open the app (App Launcher → Pledgivo → Settings) and the console lands on Get Started. It is the same checklist, except each step is checked against your org live, several have a one-click fix, and the rest jump you to the panel that owns them.

    A step there is never a box you tick. It reports what your org actually looks like right now — so if someone later deactivates the last payment account or aborts the scheduled jobs, the step reopens on its own.

    7 steps 2 are Setup-only ~20 minutes

    Permission sets#

    Nobody can see a campaign, a donation, or the Settings console without one of the packaged permission sets. The install cannot assign them for you — Salesforce gives a post-install script no way to know who on your team does what.

    Permission set Give it to
    Fundraising_Admin Whoever runs fundraising. Full CRUD on every object, plus the Settings console.
    Fundraising_User The rest of the fundraising team. Day-to-day record access, no setup.
    Fundraising_ReadOnly Anyone who needs to see gifts but never change them — finance, leadership.
    Fundraising_GuestDonor Not a person. Assign to your Experience Cloud site's guest user only. Least-privilege grants for the public donation flow.

    In Setup → Permission Sets, open each one → Manage Assignments → Add Assignment.

    The Get Started panel can assign Fundraising_Admin to you in one click, which is enough to finish the rest of setup. Your team still needs doing by hand.

    Record types#

    The app writes donations, events, and tributes onto Opportunity and Campaign record types. The install seeds a default mapping of seven records pointing at the record types it ships, so this step is normally already green.

    Change it only if your org already uses its own record types for gifts and you want the app to write to those instead. Settings console → Record Types.

    Change this before you take gifts, not after

    Remapping later does not move donations that were already written under the old record type. Decide now if you are going to.

    Experience site#

    Donors give on an Experience Cloud site, not inside Salesforce. Internal-only use — staff recording gifts taken over the phone — does not need one; anything public does.

    1. If Setup has no Digital Experiences menu, switch the feature on first: Setup → Digital Experiences → Settings → Enable Digital Experiences. It is org-wide, asks you to pick an Enhanced Domain subdomain, and cannot be undone.
    2. Settings console → Site & Domain. The panel is a seven-step checklist: create or pick your site in step 2, then work down. Steps it can verify against your org are marked Done or Pending; steps Salesforce exposes no API for are marked Can't verify and are yours to confirm.
    3. In Experience Builder, enable Settings → General → Public Access, then publish the site. Both are required — public access without publishing serves nothing, and publishing without public access serves a login page.

    A site that exists but has never been published serves nothing to the public. This is the single most common reason a donation link 404s. The Get Started panel shows the site as Almost until its status is Live.

    Full walkthrough: Set up your Experience Cloud site.

    Connect Stripe#

    This package never ships, stores, or creates a payment credential of its own. You build the External Credential and Named Credential yourself in Setup — that is where your Stripe secret key lives, encrypted, and it never touches a custom field or an Apex method.

    Settings console → Payments is the guide for that build: ten steps with the exact values to paste, each one graded against what your org actually says rather than ticked off by hand. Work down it, then add a Payment Account pointing at the finished Named Credentials. Until an active payment account exists, the donation form cannot take money.

    Full walkthrough: Connecting Stripe.

    Grant the Stripe credential#

    One assignment — deliberately left to you

    This is the step that catches people out. A credential nobody is granted is a credential nobody can use — and granting it to your Experience Cloud site's guest user widens what an anonymous visitor can reach, so it stays your decision to make. The package never does it.

    Public donations fail at the payment step until you do it. Everything else on the page works, which is exactly what makes it easy to miss.

    1. Setup → Permission Sets → the permission set you created for payments (step 7 of the Payments guide), or New if you haven't yet.
    2. On it, Apps → External Credential Principal Access → Edit → enable your external credential's principal → Save.
    3. Manage Assignments → Add Assignment → assign any fundraising staff who take payments or issue refunds. Assign your Experience Cloud site's guest user here only if this set carries the public, charge-only credential's principal — the set carrying the admin credential's principal must reach staff and nothing else.

    Don't forget the guest user — and don't give it the admin credential

    Assigning it to staff but not to the site guest user is the failure mode that looks healthiest: internal testing works, and every public donation fails at payment.

    The opposite mistake is worse and just as quiet: putting the guest user on the permission set that grants the admin credential lets an anonymous visitor's session reach a full secret key, refunds included. Step 8 of the Payments guide reports that as a failure with the remediation — see Connecting Stripe.

    Why the permission set can't be part of the package

    Salesforce does not allow a managed permission set to grant access to an external credential principal — the API rejects it outright. That's why this set has to be one you create in your own org, and why Fundraising_Admin can never ship with the grant on it.

    Once the guest user holds the assignment, the Get Started panel turns this step green.

    Background jobs#

    Donations are finalized, payments swept, refunds reconciled, and campaign totals repaired by scheduled Apex — 21 cron entries in total. The install schedules all of them.

    You only need this step if some were aborted (a deployment that touched a scheduled class will do it). Settings console → Get Started → Schedule the missing jobs, or Scheduled Jobs for the per-job breakdown and on-demand runs.

    Family Count Cadence
    Finalizer-<m> 12 Every 5 minutes
    ReconciliationHeartbeat-<m> 4 Every 15 minutes
    Recurring donations, retention purge 2 Daily
    StripePaymentSweep, RefundReconcile 2 Hourly
    CampaignRollup 1 Nightly

    Branding#

    Your organization name and logo appear on the donation form, the receipt email, and the donor portal. The install seeds a placeholder logo so nothing renders broken on day one — it is not your logo.

    Settings console → Organization & Donations: set the display name, upload your logo, and set your mailing address (receipts need it).

    Full walkthrough: Brand your receipt and notification emails.

    Third-party content#

    Optional — nothing here is required

    During the install Salesforce shows you an Approve Third-Party Access dialog listing every external domain the package wants your org to trust. This package lists Stripe and nothing else:

    Domain Why
    js.stripe.com Loads Stripe.js, which collects the card details
    api.stripe.com The payment API the browser confirms against
    m.stripe.com, m.stripe.network Stripe's fraud-signal endpoints — payments fail without them
    hooks.stripe.com 3-D Secure / bank authentication screens

    That is deliberate. A fundraising package asking to reach a list of unfamiliar domains reads as a warning rather than as a payment integration, so the optional features that use other hosts ship without their trusted sites. You add those yourself, only if you want the feature.

    Add this domain Directive Unlocks
    https://images.unsplash.com Image Using an Unsplash photo as a campaign hero or social card
    https://www.youtube.com Frame A YouTube video in a campaign page video block
    https://www.youtube-nocookie.com Frame YouTube privacy-enhanced (no-cookie) embeds
    https://player.vimeo.com Frame A Vimeo video in a campaign page video block

    To add one: Setup → Security → CSP Trusted Sites → New Trusted Site. Enter the URL exactly as written above, name it anything, tick the directive shown, leave Context as All, and save.

    The Get Started panel's Trust the content hosts you actually use step reads your org's trusted sites live and shows which of the four you have. It never counts against your 7 steps and it never offers a fix button — Salesforce does not let Apex create a trusted site, so this one is yours.

    Your campaigns start with a packaged hero image, not a stock photo

    The package ships its own artwork — a hero backdrop and a social share card, both drawn from the brand palette rather than licensed from a stock library — and publishes them into your org at install time. Your page designs are stamped with them automatically, so a campaign looks finished on day one.

    They are served from your own org's file domain, which browsers allow without a trusted site, so nothing on the list above is needed to see them. Replace either one at any time by uploading your own image in Designs; an image you have chosen is never overwritten by a package upgrade. To paste a URL from an outside host instead — Unsplash, your own CDN — that host needs a CSP trusted site first, per the table above.

    When you're done#

    The Get Started panel shows 7/7 and "Your org is ready to take donations." Setup is finished — but your org still has nothing to give to.

    Everything you raise money with lives in the Fundraisers tab#

    Campaigns and ticketed events are not created in Settings. Open the Fundraisers tab (App Launcher → Pledgivo → Fundraisers, or the Open Fundraisers button on the finished Get Started panel). It is the console your fundraising team works in day to day, and it is the only place a public donation page comes from.

    In the Fundraisers tab What you get
    New fundraiser wizard A Campaign, step by step: goal and fund, designations, page design, and its public URL.
    Ticket types Turns a campaign into a ticketed event — tiers, prices, capacity, and the registration page.
    The fundraiser list Search, filter, publish, or deactivate anything already live.
    A fundraiser's detail page Its donations, registrations, attendees, questions, and FAQs in one place.

    The Settings console you just finished is for org-wide configuration — payments, receipts, branding, jobs. You will rarely go back to it. The Fundraisers tab is where the actual work happens.

    Full walkthrough: Set up your first campaign, or Set up a ticketed event.

    Then prove it end to end#

    Once your first campaign is published, send yourself a real gift for the smallest amount Stripe allows and refund it. It is the only way to prove the whole path — form, payment, receipt, refund — works in your org.
